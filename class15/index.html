Class 15 · Optimization under uncertainty

Adaptive optimization

Decide what must be decided now, and plan how to react to every possible future. Then solve the resulting giant model by splitting it into a master problem and one small problem per scenario.

Overview

The big picture

Robust optimization made one decision that had to survive the worst case. Adaptive optimization recognizes that many decisions can wait until the uncertainty is resolved, and optimizes the expected outcome.

You book a venue for a conference months in advance, without knowing how many people will register. Catering, chairs and staff can be ordered the week before, once registrations are in. The venue is the here-and-now decision. Catering is wait-and-see. A good venue choice accounts for how expensive it will be to cater for 50, 200 or 500 people in it.

\[ \min_x\ c^\top x + \sum_s \pi_s\, Q_s(x), \qquad Q_s(x) = \min_{y_s \ge 0} \{ f_s^\top y_s : V y_s = w_s - U_s x \}. \]

Choose \(x\) now. In each scenario \(s\), react optimally with \(y_s\). Minimize today's cost plus the expected cost of reacting.

In the lecture's facility location case, planning for average demand costs $1,528M in expectation. The adaptive plan costs $1,083M, a 29% saving, mostly by avoiding shortages. Benders decomposition solves the model with 1,000 scenarios to proven optimality where the direct approach times out.

Takeaways from the lecture
  1. Adaptive optimization models sequential decision-making under uncertainty.
  2. Benders decomposition exploits the block-angular structure using duality and convexity.
  3. It alternates a master problem (first-stage solution) and a subproblem (an optimality cut).
  4. It is exact and finite, and keeps an optimality gap until convergence.
  5. Adaptive solutions add real value over one-stage benchmarks.

Two-stage model\(\min c^\top x + \sum_s \pi_s f_s^\top y_s\)
\(Ax = b,\ U_s x + V y_s = w_s\ \forall s\)
\(x\): no index \(s\). \(y_s\): one copy per scenario.
Recourse\(Q_s(x) = \max_p \{(w_s - U_s x)^\top p : V^\top p \le f_s\}\)
Convex, piecewise linear in \(x\).
BendersMaster: \(\min c^\top x + \theta\), \(\theta \ge (w - Ux)^\top q^v\) for known cuts. LB = master value. UB = \(c^\top x_t + f^\top y_t\). Stop when \(\theta_t = f^\top y_t\).
Value measuresWS \(\le\) Adaptive \(\le\) Deterministic.
VSS = Det − Adaptive. EVPI = Adaptive − WS.

Question 1 · Modeling
Bioreactor lines \(x_i \in \{0,1\}\) must be leased before profits are known. Production \(y_{ij}\) and product volumes \(z_k\) can wait. In the two-stage model with scenarios \(s\), which variables get a scenario index?
Leasing is here and now, so \(x_i\) stays scenario-independent. Production and formulation adapt to each scenario: \(y_{ijs}\), \(z_{ks}\).

Question 5 · Value
Which inequality always holds for expected costs in a minimization problem?
The deterministic first-stage plan is one feasible choice for the adaptive model, so adaptive is at most deterministic. Wait and see relaxes non-anticipativity, so it is at most adaptive.
Question 6 · Interpretation
A planner finds EVPI is large but VSS is zero. What does that mean?
VSS = 0: the adaptive and deterministic first stages perform equally. Large EVPI: committing before the uncertainty resolves is costly, so information (or delaying the decision) has value.
