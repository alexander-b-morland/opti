A tour of non-linear models

Non-linearity enters models in a handful of recurring ways: through norms that measure error or distance, through products of variables, and through curved relationships like risk. This part walks through the lecture's examples.

Why non-linear?

Non-linear optimization (NLO) covers problems with a non-linear objective, non-linear constraints, or both. It can describe system dynamics that linear models cannot, which is why it shows up in machine learning (logistic regression, neural networks), geometry and computer vision, finance, energy, manufacturing, transportation and everyday technology such as electric-vehicle charging and recommendation systems.

Which algorithm works depends on the structure: is the problem constrained or not, and what form do the objective and constraints take? Above all, is it convex? That question drives parts b and c.

The approximation problem

Formulation
\[ \min_x\ \big\{ \|Ax - b\| : x \in X \big\} \]

Find \(x\) so that \(Ax\) is as close as possible to a target \(b\). The norm decides what "close" means.

NameObjectiveWhat it punishes
Least squares (2-norm)\(\min \sum_i (a_i^\top x - b_i)^2\)Large errors heavily, because they are squared
Absolute residuals (1-norm)\(\min \sum_i |a_i^\top x - b_i|\)All errors in proportion. Robust to outliers.
Minimax (\(\infty\)-norm)\(\min \max_i |a_i^\top x - b_i|\)Only the single worst error

The same template covers model fitting (approximate a response \(b\) by \(Ax\)), geometry (project \(b\) onto the subspace spanned by the columns of \(A\)) and engineering (a design \(x\) with minimal deviation from a target \(b\)).

Each line is the best fit for its norm. Drag the outlier up and down, then drag other points.

The 1-norm line barely notices the outlier. The 2-norm line tilts towards it. The \(\infty\)-norm line is decided entirely by the most extreme points, so a single outlier dominates it. Choosing a norm is a modeling decision about which errors matter.

Regression and denoising

Features \(x_{ij}\), targets \(y_i\). Find coefficients \(\beta\) such that \(\sum_j \beta_j x_{ij} \approx y_i\). Two refinements make it non-trivial: regularization \(R(\beta)\) keeps the coefficients well behaved (for example \(R(\beta) = \lambda\sum_j \beta_j^2\)), and weights \(w_i\) give less importance to unreliable points such as outliers.

\[ \min_\beta\ \sum_{i=1}^n w_i \Big( y_i - \sum_{j=1}^m x_{ij}\beta_j \Big)^2 + R(\beta). \]

Now \(y_i \in \{0, 1\}\). Model the probability with the logistic function:

\[ p_i = \mathbb{P}(y_i = 1 \mid x_i) = \frac{e^{\beta^\top x_i}}{1 + e^{\beta^\top x_i}}. \]

We want \(p_i\) high when \(y_i = 1\) and low when \(y_i = 0\). Maximize the regularized log-likelihood:

\[ \max_\beta\ \sum_{i=1}^n \Big( y_i\, \beta^\top x_i - \log\big(1 + e^{\beta^\top x_i}\big) \Big) - R(\beta). \]

(Written as a maximum, the regularizer enters with a minus sign: it penalizes large coefficients.)

You observe \(b = x + w\), a signal \(x\) plus unknown noise \(w\). Balance two goals: stay close to the observations, and avoid jumps between consecutive values (the true signal is smooth over time or space):

\[ \min_x\ \|x - b\|^2 + \lambda \sum_{i} (x_i - x_{i+1})^2. \]

\(\lambda\) sets the trade-off: larger values give smoother, less faithful reconstructions.

Geometry

ProblemFormulation
Project a point \(x_0\) onto a set \(X\)\(\min \{\|x - x_0\| : x \in X\}\)
Distance between two sets\(\min \{\|x - y\| : x \in X,\ y \in Y\}\)
Largest ellipsoid inside a polyhedron \(P\)\(\max \log|\det D|\) s.t. \(\mathcal{E}(D, d) \subseteq P\), where \(\mathcal{E}(D, d) = \{x : (x - d)^\top D^{-2}(x - d) \le 1\}\)

The volume of the ellipsoid is proportional to \(|\det D|\). Maximizing its logarithm instead makes the problem concave in \(D\), a hint of why reformulation matters in non-linear optimization.

Portfolio optimization

Invest a budget \(B\) in \(n\) stocks. Returns \(r_j\) are random, but we can estimate their means \(\mu_j\), variances \(\sigma_{jj} = \mathrm{Var}(r_j)\) and covariances \(\sigma_{ij} = \mathrm{Cov}(r_i, r_j)\). Let \(x_j\) be the amount invested in stock \(j\). Minimize risk (variance) subject to a target return \(\rho\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \min\ & \sum_{i=1}^n x_i^2\, \mathrm{Var}(r_i) + \sum_{i \ne j} x_i x_j\, \sigma_{ij} \;=\; x^\top \Sigma x \\ \text{s.t. } & \sum_i x_i \le B,\qquad \sum_i \mu_i x_i \ge \rho,\qquad x \ge 0. \end{aligned} \]

The constraints are linear (budget, return target, no short sales). The non-linearity comes from the risk term: variance is quadratic in the investments, and the covariances reward diversification.

Locating a power plant

Demand nodes \(i = 1, \dots, m\) sit at locations \(c_i \in \mathbb{R}^2\) with demand weights \(w_i\). Where should a new plant \(x\) go? Two natural objectives give two classic problems.

Fermat-Weber problem
\[ \min_x \sum_{i=1}^m w_i \|x - c_i\| \quad \text{s.t. } Ax \le b \]

Minimize total weighted distance. Efficient on average.

Ball circumscription
\[ \min_{x,\, \rho}\ \rho \quad \text{s.t. } \|x - c_i\| \le \rho\ \ \forall i \]

Minimize the largest distance. Fair to the worst-served customer.

Interactive

Sum of distances versus maximum distance

Equal weights. One customer is far away from the rest. See how differently the two objectives react to it.

The far customer barely moves the Fermat-Weber point, which stays with the cluster. It drags the minimax center halfway out, because the minimax objective only cares about the worst case. The same contrast as between the 1-norm and the \(\infty\)-norm fits above.

Floor planning

Place \(n\) rectangles in a bounding box of width \(W\) and height \(H\), minimizing its perimeter. Each rectangle has a lower-left corner \((x_i, y_i)\), a width \(w_i\), a height \(h_i\), and a reference point \((u_i, v_i)\) inside it. The relative order is given: \((i, j) \in \mathcal{H}\) means \(i\) is left of \(j\), and \((i, j) \in \mathcal{V}\) means \(i\) is below \(j\).

\[ \begin{aligned} \min\ & 2(W + H) \\ \text{s.t. } & x_i \ge 0,\ y_i \ge 0,\ x_i + w_i \le W,\ y_i + h_i \le H && \text{inside the box} \\ & x_i + w_i + \delta \le x_j\ \ (i, j) \in \mathcal{H},\qquad y_i + h_i + \delta \le y_j\ \ (i, j) \in \mathcal{V} && \text{no overlap, buffer } \delta \\ & w_i h_i \ge A_i,\qquad l_i \le h_i / w_i \le u_i && \text{area and aspect ratio} \\ & x_i \le u_i \le x_i + w_i,\ \ y_i \le v_i \le y_i + h_i,\ \ \|(u_i, v_i) - (u_j, v_j)\| \le D_{ij} && \text{distances} \end{aligned} \]

The area constraint \(w_i h_i \ge A_i\) multiplies two variables: that is the non-linearity. (Here \(u_i\) appears both as an upper bound on the aspect ratio and as a reference coordinate. The lecture reuses the symbol.)

Pickups in ride-sharing

A vehicle drives from origin \(O\) to destination \(D\) at speed \(S\). Customer \(i\) starts at home \(H_i\), walks at speed \(S_i\), and can walk at most \(W_i\) to a pickup point. Where and when should each pickup happen to minimize arrival time? Stop locations \(M_i \in \mathbb{R}^2\) and times \(v_i\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \min\ & v_{n+1} \\ \text{s.t. } & v_0 = 0,\ M_0 = O,\ M_{n+1} = D \\ & v_i \ge v_{i-1} + \|M_i - M_{i-1}\| / S, && i = 1, \dots, n + 1 \\ & v_i \ge \|M_i - H_i\| / S_i, && i = 1, \dots, n \\ & \|M_i - H_i\| \le W_i, && i = 1, \dots, n. \end{aligned} \]

The vehicle cannot arrive before driving there, and cannot leave before the customer has walked there. The norms make it non-linear, but in a convex way.

Summary

  • Norms of errors or distances: approximation, regression, location, geometry, routing.
  • Quadratic forms: risk in portfolios, regularization, smoothing.
  • Products of variables: areas in floor planning.
  • The choice of norm or objective encodes a modeling judgment: average performance, robustness to outliers, or the worst case.
Check yourself
Quick check
You fit a line to data with a few wild outliers that you believe are recording errors. Which approximation is the most sensible?
The 1-norm penalizes errors linearly, so a few large residuals do not dominate. Squaring (2-norm) or taking the maximum (\(\infty\)-norm) gives outliers much more influence.
